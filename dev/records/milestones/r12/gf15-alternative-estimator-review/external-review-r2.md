## Findings

### ext2-1 [major]
- section: Validation regime — **D6: Other control contracts.**
- finding: The new forward-CDF round-trip control fixes an **absolute probability** tolerance of 1e−10 and applies it to "every analytical/branch reference case", including the D5 rows with t₃ ≤ −.969999. At those rows the GEV density at the p=.9 quantile is O(10⁶–10⁸) in normalized units, so a single float64 unit in the last place of q̂ᵧ already moves F(q̂ᵧ) by 10⁻⁹–10⁻⁸. The control is therefore unsatisfiable as written for four of its own frozen reference rows, unless q̂ᵧ is not the adapter's float64 output — in which case the control does not exercise the candidate at all.
- rationale: The round-trip error obeys Δp = f(q)·Δq, and the design supplies every term. For the D5 row t₃=−.98 with l₁=0, l₂=1: the oracle root of τ(c)=−.98 is c≈6.6, so by D1's own inversions σᵧ=l₂c/[Γ(1+c)(1−2⁻ᶜ)]≈2.9e−3 and μᵧ≈1.01. With u=−log p=0.105361, the GEV density at the p=.9 quantile is f=p·u^(1−c)/σᵧ≈9e7, while one ulp of q̂ᵧ≈1.01 is 2.2e−16, giving Δp≈2e−8 — about 200× the stated tolerance. For t₃=−.97/−.970001/−.969999 (c≈5.94, σᵧ≈9.4e−3) f≈6.5e6 and Δp≈1.4e−9, still >1e−10. Meeting 1e−10 at these rows would require q̂ᵧ accurate to ~1e−18 absolute, i.e. far below float64 resolution at magnitude 1. This is an arithmetic property of the quantile/probability Jacobian, not an implementation defect; note that the *retained* control is scaled in quantile space (1e−10 × max(1,|qᵧ|)), whereas the new one is unscaled in probability space with no Jacobian allowance. Observable consequence: D6 states "failed round-trip stops readiness", so Stage 1 halts on these enumerated rows and the qualification cannot reach Stage 2 — or the executor silently rescales a frozen tolerance mid-flight, exactly the behaviour the design forbids elsewhere ("Any proposed change to an original analytical tolerance returns to design review"). Under the alternative reading, where F is evaluated on the oracle's own high-precision quantile, the identity is self-satisfying to ~1e−40 and ext1-8's concern (an independent check on the candidate's returned quantile) remains unaddressed for the candidate. I checked the four Newton/alternate-start rows and the population and rational rows: only t₃ ≤ −.969999 fail; the c={−.2,0,.2} population cases give Δp ~1e−17 and the t₃=−.8/−.85 rows ~1e−13, so the fix is narrow.
- suggested_fix: State explicitly whose quantile enters the round trip (adapter float64 output, which is the useful test), and replace the flat probability tolerance with one that carries the Jacobian — either compare in quantile space with the existing relative form, or keep probability space with an explicit allowance such as |F(q̂ᵧ)−p| ≤ 1e−10 + k·f(q̂ᵧ)·ulp(q̂ᵧ) for a fixed small k, with f evaluated by the same high-precision reference. Freeze the per-row expected bound in the Stage 1 characterization table rather than one global constant.

### ext2-2 [minor]
- section: Validation regime — **D5: Analytical and branch readiness.**
- finding: The branch row `Positive rational | .50, τ(.2)` and the retained c=+.2 population control evaluate the *same* library quantity: the inversion depends only on t₃, so both produce an identical |Δc|. That number is simultaneously declared "characterization, not a new per-branch accuracy gate" and a gating 1e−5 control, and the design does not acknowledge the overlap.
- rationale: In `_lmom_fit` the shape is a function of `lmom_ratios[2]` alone; l₁ and l₂ affect only loc/scale. So a hypothetical 1.5e−5 shape discrepancy at t₃=τ(.2) would be reported as an explained approximation property in the branch table while stopping readiness through the population control, with no stated precedence between the two dispositions — a foreseeable dispute at the readiness handoff the design asks the model-validator to sign. (Evaluating the pinned rational coefficients by hand at Z=1−τ(.2) gives G−0.2 ≈ 1e−7, and at Z=1−τ(0) gives |G| ≈ 1.3e−7 — i.e. the snap fires and the retained 1e−5 controls look attainable — so the practical risk is low and the author's refusal to pre-emptively loosen the tolerance is well founded; the documentation inconsistency remains.)
- suggested_fix: Note in D5 that the τ(±.2) shape discrepancy is gated by the retained population controls and is not merely characterized, and require Stage 1 to compute those two values first so the frozen characterization table is written with the gate outcome already known.

### ext2-3 [minor]
- section: Validation regime — **D5: Analytical and branch readiness.**
- finding: The validation-only shape oracle is specified as bracketed bisection that "expand[s] the upper bracket until a sign change", but no lower bracket endpoint is given, and the stated domain (c > −1) excludes the only natural choice.
- rationale: τ(c) decreases monotonically from τ(−1)=1 to −1 as c→∞, so for any admissible |t₃|<1 a bracket of the form [−1, c_hi] with upward expansion is correct; but an implementer freezing the oracle at Stage 1 has to rediscover that the endpoint may be taken *at* c=−1 (where τ is exactly 1 and the sign is strict) even though the fitted domain is open there. Left unstated, a bracket starting at, say, −0.99 silently fails for t₃ > τ(−0.99), which includes samples plausibly produced at n=10 with c=−.2. The consequence is a readiness-stage oracle defect rather than a matrix error, which is why this is minor.
- suggested_fix: State the initial bracket ([−1, c_hi] with τ(−1)=1 as the exact upper-sign endpoint), the expansion rule for c_hi, and the behaviour if no sign change is found within a stated bound.
