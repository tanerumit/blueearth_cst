Decisions behind the wording

  • Separate construction, execution, and evaluation. A model built from global data is not calibrated locally. A successful simulation is not evidence of fit to observations. Evaluation figures may be simulation-only when observations are absent.
  • Name the actual product. The spatial foundation precedes Wflow parameterization; model forcing differs from the extracted historical climate grid. Outlet identities are not necessarily observation stations, and the map display reference is not an observational truth.
  • Explain legitimate omissions. Preserve waterbody setup reasons, optional-output choices, missing observations, and short-record restrictions. A missing source file does not prove that no waterbody exists in the basin. A missing required spatial variable can fail a declared-output rule.
  • Describe warm-up accurately. The plotting code excludes the first calendar year when the discharge series has more than 365 time stamps; otherwise it retains warm-up in summaries. These messages describe postprocessing, not a new simulation or a change to exported model output.
  • Preserve identity and coverage. Keep station IDs, duplicate-series decisions, source names, dates, output paths, warning severity, and full coverage. Readable prose should not hide gauge-discovery diagnostics or silently sample a long station list.
  • Keep upstream vocabulary and records. Wflow-SBM, HydroMT setup names, subprocess commands, native exceptions, and the Wflow progress protocol retain their technical meaning. The proposed text changes the surrounding explanation, not physics, source handling, or failure status.
