# WF2 published figure examples

**47 original figure/table images from 20 publications, grouped into 27 analysis dimensions.** Open the
[offline visual gallery](gallery.html) in a browser, or use the section indexes below. Each index includes
image previews, citations, figure numbers, interpretation, regional adaptation notes and reuse terms.
Images were downloaded from publishers at their available figure resolution, unchanged, rather than captured
as lower-resolution screenshots. Click a gallery image to open the full file.

The numbering follows the [expanded research guide](../wf2-projections-research-guide.md). The
[original idea note](../wf2-climate-projections-ideas.md) uses different numbering; see the crosswalk below.
This is a curated design reference collection, not climate findings for a particular project or a claim that
every published design is optimal for every audience.

## Selection and reading route

Recent research examples include constrained regional variability diagnostics and coherent extreme outcomes
from 2026, large-ensemble diagnostics and overshoot pathways from 2025. Established examples contribute
communication studies, applied catchment layouts, weighting diagnostics and bottom-up response surfaces.
Selection prioritizes methodological relevance, readable regional presentation, inspectable original captions
and accessible figure files. It is not an exhaustive literature search or a ranking of all available graphics.

For a broad-audience regional summary, start with **2 → 4/C1 → 4/C2 → 4/C5 → 6.2 → 9 → 10**. For an analytical
review, also examine coverage, distribution diagnostics, sensitivity, weighting and spatial processing.
The gallery contains all examples; its navigation jumps directly to each dimension.

## Interpretation and coverage limits

All substantive analysis dimensions have a figure example, but some fine-grained ideas require adapting a
related design rather than copying an exact published diagnostic:

- **Coverage:** C0 provides an archive inventory. A requested/resolved/excluded coverage matrix needs to be
  designed from the suite's own run records.
- **Scenario contrasts:** C5 illustrates readable agreement and separate scenario summaries. It does not show
  paired within-model scenario differences on common membership. Add that diagnostic before attributing a
  difference to scenario choice.
- **Persistence and sampling:** C4 includes distribution and rank diagnostics, and C6 includes record-length
  sensitivity. There is no dedicated lag-profile example or temporal block-bootstrap graphic here. The 5.2
  example concerns ensemble-member subsampling, which does not validate a temporal resampling method.
- **Fixed thresholds:** the daily example concerns Rx5day intensity. Reference-event frequency appears in 7.2;
  there is no separate local heat-exposure exceedance chart or fitted GEV diagnostic panel in this collection.
- **Emergence:** 6.3 illustrates global observational detection. Its threshold display is transferable; its
  emergence dates and fitted detection method are not local projection results.
- **Spatial support:** 8.1 includes catchment context and national grid-cell distributions. It does not show
  a catchment mean, a subcatchment extreme and a population-weighted national mean on the same dataset. Add
  that matched support comparison when operationalizing the spatial-scale questions.
- **CST:** the response-surface example uses inflow volume and dispersion. Its geometry and explanation are
  useful, but WF2 temperature/precipitation factors cannot be placed directly on those axes. Use identical
  stressors, transformations and reference definitions on the surface and projection overlay.
- **Communication:** a visually attractive published figure is an example to examine. Audience comprehension
  needs testing in the regional communication context; confidence and preference are different outcomes.

Hydrological examples are explicitly marked in their adaptation notes. They provide useful catchment layouts,
but monthly climate inputs cannot reproduce daily hazards or hydrological responses without additional data
and modeling. Percentiles and density shapes have to identify their sampling unit: models, initial-condition
members, years, events or grid cells. An ensemble range is not a bound on all possible future conditions.

## Sources, attribution and reuse

Use [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md) for the bibliography, [sources.json](sources.json) for source-level metadata, and
[manifest.json](manifest.json) for every file's original image URL, figure ID, citation, interpretation note,
rights statement, retrieval date, dimensions, byte size and SHA-256 digest. The six additional publications
extend the guide with applied visual exemplars; its R-numbered references remain linked in the metadata.

Most included papers license their material under
[Creative Commons Attribution 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Retain author, paper, figure
number and license attribution when reusing those images. An article's license does not automatically cover
separately credited third-party content.

**This collection has mixed reuse terms.** The IPCC image is retained unchanged under the site's limited
personal, non-commercial reproduction conditions. The two AMS/Dunning images have unverified redistribution
terms, and Shepherd Figure 1 includes separately credited photographs. Keep these as personal research
references unless the required reuse rights are established. Do not publish the whole collection under a
blanket CC license. Relevant qualifications are repeated beside the affected images. IPCC conditions are
documented on its [copyright page](https://www.ipcc.ch/copyright/); the AMS reuse-policy page was blocked when
checked. No publication or external redistribution is part of this task.

## Verification record

All 47 downloaded files decode as images. Their dimensions and hashes were recorded, and all figures were
visually inspected on contact sheets, with selected detailed checks. Local index, gallery and manifest links
were checked against the saved files. Checks cover collection integrity and figure-to-caption matching; they
do not validate the original studies' numerical results or establish permissions beyond the recorded terms.
